# OctoAcme Project Management

OctoAcme manages cross-functional projects through a lightweight lifecycle of initiation, planning, execution, release, and continuous improvement. Work begins with a one-pager that defines the problem, desired outcome, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once sponsors and stakeholders agree on the priority and the team has capacity, the initiative moves into planning, where the team creates a prioritized backlog, estimates work, defines acceptance criteria and the Definition of Done, maps dependencies, and agrees on release milestones.

Delivery is iterative and visible. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, and reporting; Product Managers define outcomes and prioritize customer value; developers design, build, review, test, and document the solution; QA validates acceptance criteria and critical flows; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Teams use project boards, small pull requests, CI checks, code review, demos, and delivery metrics to track progress and maintain quality from implementation through staged deployment and post-release verification.

Communication keeps these roles aligned: delivery teams surface progress and blockers in standups, Project and Product Managers synchronize weekly, stakeholders receive regular status or milestone updates, and urgent issues follow a clear escalation path. A shared project README or release document acts as the source of truth, while risk registers, decision logs, dashboards, and incident updates make dependencies, decisions, and operational signals visible.

Quality assurance is built into every phase through clear acceptance criteria, unit and integration tests, critical-flow smoke tests, manual QA where needed, automated linting and security scans, required review, and rollback readiness. After sprints, releases, milestones, and incidents, teams hold blameless retrospectives, assign a small number of improvement actions with owners and due dates, and track their impact in the backlog.
